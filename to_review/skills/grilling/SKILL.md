# Grilling

Interview me relentlessly about every aspect of this until we reach a shared
understanding. Walk down each branch of the decision tree, resolving dependencies
between decisions one-by-one. For each question, provide your recommended answer.

Ask the questions one at a time, waiting for feedback on each question before
continuing. Asking multiple questions at once is bewildering.

If a fact can be found by exploring the environment (filesystem, tools, etc.),
look it up rather than asking me. The decisions, though, are mine — put each one to
me and wait for my answer.

Do not act on it until I confirm we have reached a shared understanding.

---

## What is worth taking from this

Notes for whoever mines it. Nothing here has been decided.

**One question at a time, waiting between them.** The strongest idea in the file
and the one most often got wrong. A batch of questions gets a batch of shallow
answers, or none — the owner picks the two he finds interesting and the rest
quietly become assumptions. This belongs in whatever governs how the agent asks
for decisions.

**Every question carries a recommended answer.** Turns an interrogation into a
review: the owner can accept, correct, or reject, and correcting is far cheaper
than composing. The same principle already appears in the review survey, where
proposals arrive as options with a recommendation — evidence it generalises.

**Look it up rather than asking.** A clean division: facts are the agent's job,
decisions are the owner's. Asking about something discoverable spends the owner's
attention on the one thing he cannot delegate.

**Walk the decision tree, resolving dependencies in order.** Answering a dependent
question before the one it depends on produces an answer that has to be revisited.
Related to ordering decisions by how much rests on them.

**"Do not act until I confirm shared understanding."** An explicit stop. Stronger
than "ask before building" because it names the condition that ends the waiting.

### Where it might land

Extending `working-agreement` is the obvious candidate — it already covers the
order work goes in and the division of decision rights, and these are the same
subject at a finer grain. It is currently in the review zone itself.

The competing reading is that grilling is its own thing: a deliberate mode the
owner enters when he wants his thinking attacked, not a rule that always applies.
On that reading it stays a separate skill, invoked on purpose, and only the
question-shape ideas migrate into the working agreement.

That choice is the owner's and has not been made.
