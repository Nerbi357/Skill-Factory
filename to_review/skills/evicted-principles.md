# Evicted principles

Rules that were true and useful but sat in the wrong skill. Each was removed under
the gate **one skill, one task or one principle**, and each is parked here rather
than deleted, because the rule was never the problem — its address was.

This file is raw material, not a skill. Entries leave it by being folded into an
existing skill where they genuinely belong, or by becoming the seed of a new one.
An entry that nothing ever claims is itself a finding: the principle was probably
weaker than it looked.

**Format:** what it says · where it came from · why it was evicted · where it might
go.

---

## Check the free option before the paid one, and check whether it already exists

Before building something, look for whether it already exists and is maintained.
Before paying, look for the free path. Finding a published artefact that already
does the job is often the single highest-value hour available, and it is
frequently skipped because building feels more like progress than searching does.

## End with what you would do

Research exists to support a decision, and marks alone do not make one. A report
scrupulously honest about every fact and silent about what to do with them hands
the entire judgement back untouched — the failure mode this discipline invites,
because carefulness feels like completion.

Finish with three lines: **what I would do on this evidence**; **what it rests
on** — which of the marked facts it actually depends on; and **what would change
it** — the single check that would most move the answer. The third is the one that
pays, because it turns an open question into a task with a known cost.

When the evidence genuinely does not support a recommendation, that *is* the
recommendation — say it outright rather than leaving it to be inferred from a wall
of qualified facts.
